Why Search Console and GA4 can't see AI answers

Only 12% of Google AI Mode citations match a page in the organic top 10. Learn how to track your brand’s AI share of voice and turn the gaps into a fix list.

Vamsi Krishna13 October 2026AI visibility audit, measure GEO performance, LLM brand monitoring

Traditional SEO tools measure two things: where you rank and who clicks. AI answers break both.

There's no rank to track: An AI engine doesn't return ten blue links. It writes a paragraph, and your brand is either in it or not. The same question asked twice can produce two different answers, so there's no fixed position to monitor.

There's often no click: So for instance, if ChatGPT or Gemini recommends three tools (and lists the name of each tool), the user may directly click on one of the listed tools or later search for the brand name of one of the tools in a regular search. The visit from that user will show up in your analytics as either direct traffic or as branded traffic but not be tied back to the AI answer that triggered the recommendation in the first place. We covered the broader issue of zero-click search in a prior post.

Google's own AI features don't help much either. : Traffic from Google’s AI Overview and AI Mode features in Google Search is reported in your normal web traffic in Search Console and can’t be split out to report how many times you appeared in the AI-generated summary versus normal search results.

The upshot: if you want to know how AI engines talk about your brand, you have to ask them directly and record what they say. That's what an AI share of voice audit does.

The five numbers that actually matter

"Are we in ChatGPT?" is a yes/no question. It's a starting point, but it won't tell you much. Most single "AI visibility scores" also blend several different signals into one number, a problem Search Engine Journal flagged in its post on how brands measure AI visibility. Track these five separately instead.

Metric

What it measures

How to calculate it

Mention rate

How often your brand appears in answers at all

Answers that name you ÷ total answers

Citation rate

How often the AI links to or sources your own pages

Answers citing your domain ÷ total answers

Answer position

Where you land when you are mentioned

Average rank in the list (1st named, 2nd named, and so on)

Sentiment and accuracy

Whether what it says about you is positive and correct

Tag each mention: positive, neutral, negative, or factually wrong

Share of voice

Your visibility against competitors

Your mentions ÷ total brand mentions across the prompt set

Mention rate and citation rate are different things, and the gap between them tells you a lot. A brand with a high mention rate but low citation rate is being talked about, but the AI is getting its information from somewhere else, usually a review site, a Reddit thread or a competitor's comparison page. You don't control that narrative.

Share of voice is the number leadership will care about. The standard formula, as Semrush lays out in its guide to measuring AI share of voice, is your brand's mentions divided by total mentions across all brands in your category. It turns a pile of answers into one figure you can track month over month and compare against the two or three competitors that matter.

Step 1: Build a prompt set your buyers would actually type

Your audit is only as good as the questions you ask. The most common mistake we see is testing branded prompts like "What is [Brand]? " That tells you whether the AI knows you exist. It doesn't tell you whether it recommends you to someone who's never heard of you.

Build your prompt set around four types of question:

  1. Category discovery. "Best [category] for [use case]." Example: "best accounting software for small manufacturers in India".

  2. Problem-led. The buyer describes a pain, not a product. "How do I stop losing leads between my website and my sales team?"

  3. Comparison: "[Your brand] vs [competitor]" and "alternatives to [competitor]". The second one matters more than most teams realise.

  4. Decision detail. Pricing, integrations, eligibility, reviews. "Which credit cards give lounge access with no annual fee?"

The set should contain around 30-50 single prompts. Too few, and one odd answer throws off your averages. And if you have more than 100 single prompts, the manual work does not really have any value unless you are using a tracking tool as well.

Here are two insights from testing this for clients. First, prompt a chatbot much as one would converse with it – longer and more specific than a typical Google search query. Second, use real language from your sales calls, support tickets, and questions posed on subreddits and Quora in your industry. These are the types of questions that chatbot AI engines are currently being asked.


Step 2: Run the audit across engines

Don't test one engine and assume the rest behave the same way. They don't. ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google's AI Overviews pull from different sources and weigh them differently. We saw this firsthand when we asked five AI engines the same question. A brand can do well in one and be invisible in another.

A few rules keep the results honest:

  • Use a clean session. Log out, or use a fresh account with memory and personalisation switched off. Otherwise the AI may lean on your own past chats.

  • Run each prompt more than once. AI answers vary between runs. Three runs per prompt per engine gives you a fair read without tripling your workload for nothing.

  • Set the location. If you sell in India, test from India. Recommendations for "best health insurance" look very different in Mumbai and Texas.

  • Log everything in one sheet. One row per prompt, per engine, per run. Columns for: mentioned (yes/no), position, cited URL, competitors named, sentiment, and any factual errors.

  • Screenshot the odd ones. When an answer gets your pricing wrong or recommends a discontinued product, save it. That's evidence you'll need when you go to fix it.

On cadence: run a full baseline once, then repeat monthly. AI answers shift as models update and new content gets indexed, so a single audit goes stale fast. If you're tracking more than 100 prompts, paid tools can take over the repetitive part. 

Step 3: Read the citations, not just the mentions

This is where most of the useful findings come from. Engines like Perplexity, Gemini and ChatGPT with search turned on show the sources behind their answers. Sort those sources into buckets and a pattern usually jumps out.

Source type

What it tells you

Your own site

Your content is being trusted. Note which pages get cited and which never do.

Competitor sites

A rival's comparison or "alternatives" page is shaping the answer, often in their favour.

Review and listing sites

Aggregators are doing the recommending. Your profile there matters more than you think.

Reddit, Quora and forums

Real user discussion is carrying weight. If you're absent from those threads, so is your side of the story.

News and publications

Earned media is feeding the answer. PR and AI visibility are now the same conversation.

In our audits for BFSI and fintech brands, the forum bucket is often bigger than the client expects. A three-year-old Reddit thread can outweigh a polished product page because it reads like an honest answer from a real person. The data backs this up: when Semrush analysed 248,000 Reddit posts cited in AI search, most of the cited posts had fewer than 20 upvotes. You don't need a viral thread to shape the answer, just a relevant one.

Also look at what's missing. If the AI never cites your pricing page, check whether it's crawlable, whether it uses JSON-LD structured data, and whether the key facts sit in plain text rather than inside an image or a script. Slow, script-heavy pages are a common culprit here, which we covered in our post on site speed and architecture as a ranking factor.

Step 4: Turn the findings into a fix list

An audit that ends in a spreadsheet hasn't done its job yet. Match each pattern you found to a specific action.

What you found

What to do about it

Not mentioned in category prompts

Build topical authority around the category. Publish the guides and comparisons the AI is currently borrowing from others.

Mentioned, but rarely cited

Make your pages easier to quote: clear answers near the top, structured answers instead of old-style FAQ pages, and facts in plain text.

Competitor pages shape the answer

Publish your own honest comparison and alternatives pages. Don't leave that ground to a rival.

Forums dominate the citations

Show up where the conversation is, transparently and within each platform's rules. Answer real questions on Reddit and Quora rather than posting promotions.

Wrong facts about your brand

Update the source pages the AI is citing, correct your listings, and make sure your own site states the facts clearly.

Low position despite mentions

Strengthen third-party proof: reviews, press coverage and high-authority backlinks earned the right way.

Prioritise by revenue, not by volume. A gap on a high-intent decision prompt ("which plan should I pick") is worth more than a gap on a broad awareness prompt. Fix those first, then re-run the same prompts next month to see what moved.
